# Ophthalmic Emergencies & Red Flag Checklist

An emergency clinical triage guide for distinguishing sight-threatening ocular crises from benign conditions.

---

## 1. Urgent Triage: Red Eye Decision Matrix

| Sign / Symptom | Benign Conjunctivitis | Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma | Anterior Uveitis (Iritis) | Corneal Ulcer / Keratitis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pain Level** | Mild irritation, grittiness, stinging. | **Severe, excruciating, deep orbital ache.** | Moderate to severe deep throbbing pain. | Sharp pain, intense foreign body sensation. |
| **Visual Acuity** | **Normal** (unaffected by blinking). | **Severely reduced**, blurry with halos. | Mild to moderate reduction. | Reduced (depending on ulcer location). |
| **Discharge** | Watery (viral) or purulent/crusty (bacterial). | Watery tearing only; no purulent discharge. | Watery tearing only. | Watery to mucopurulent. |
| **Cornea** | Clear and transparent. | **Steamy, cloudy, hazy.** | Clear (keratic precipitates on endothelium). | **Focal white/gray opacity or infiltrate.** |
| **Pupil** | Normal size, reactive to light. | **Mid-dilated, fixed, oval, non-reactive.** | Constricted (miotic), sluggish response. | Normal or reactive. |
| **Intraocular Pressure** | Normal (10–21 mmHg). | **Markedly elevated (40–80 mmHg).** | Normal or low (or elevated in uveitic glaucoma). | Normal. |
| **Action** | Primary care / topical lubricants/antibiotics. | **Immediate ER ophthalmology consult.** | Urgent same-day ophthalmologist referral. | Urgent same-day ophthalmologist referral. |
